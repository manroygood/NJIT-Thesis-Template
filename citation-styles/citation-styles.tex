\begin{center}\setstretch{1}\normalsize\bfseries
CITATION STYLES FOR NJIT THESES AND DISSERTATIONS
\end{center}
\vspace{12pt}

\noindent Each of the following pages is devoted to one style. It lists the style's main distinguishing features, says how to type your sources in \texttt{references.bib} for it, and shows how two citations look in the text: a journal article, and a single page of a book, typed \verb+\cite[23]{Lamport1994}+. It ends with the reference list the style produces from the same sources from the template's \texttt{references.bib} on every page: two books, a journal article, an article with many authors, a chapter in a book, a dissertation, software documentation, and a website. Compare the pages for the styles your discipline accepts, and ask your advisor which one your field prefers. The citations are typed with \verb+\cite+, as in the sample dissertation. Some styles need another command or more care; their pages say so under Special Instructions, so read them before you choose.

Choose your citation style early, before you write much of the text, because the style changes how you write sentences that mention a source. In a style that cites with superscript numbers, such as AMA, or ACS and AIP when they cite with \verb+\autocite+, the number is not a word of the sentence: you cannot write ``In Reference\textsuperscript{5} it is shown that \ldots'' or ``as shown in\textsuperscript{5}''. You must name the authors instead, as in ``Knuth and Plass\textsuperscript{5} showed that \ldots''. In an author-year style, such as APA, Chicago, or Harvard, the authors' names are already part of the citation, and \verb+\textcite+ makes them part of the sentence: ``Knuth and Plass (1981) showed that \ldots''. Changing to a style of a different kind late in the work means rewriting these sentences by hand throughout the dissertation.

To use a style, set \texttt{style=} on the \texttt{biblatex} line in \texttt{packages.tex} to the value in Table~1, and compile the thesis again; nothing else changes. Chicago is the exception: replace the \texttt{biblatex} line as the comment in \texttt{packages.tex} shows. The ACM, AIP, AMA, ASCE, and TRB styles come with the template, because \texttt{biblatex} has none of its own. So do Harvard and Vancouver styles that capitalize titles as their guides ask; the Vancouver style also separates the authors with commas only.

A style can format only what is in \texttt{references.bib}. Type titles in title case and protect proper nouns and acronyms with braces, as in \texttt{\{NumPy\}}, so that styles which change capitalization keep them; write journal names out in full; and fill in every field a style needs. A style can change a title typed in title case to sentence case, but not the other way around, so title case works for every style. Each page repeats this advice and says what that style does to titles. Appendix~B of the sample points to the official guide for each style, so you can check the output.
